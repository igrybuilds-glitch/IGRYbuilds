How much does a website cost in India?

2026-10-21 · by Manmohan Sharma · ~4 min read

Ask ten people what a website costs in India and you'll get ten different answers — a few thousand rupees, tens of thousands, sometimes a lakh or more. All of them can be honest answers, because "a website" isn't one thing. A one-page site for a tailor and a booking system for a clinic are different jobs with different prices. Here's what you're actually paying for, so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

What the money goes into

A website has five cost centres, and every quote you receive is some combination of these:

Design. Custom design built for your business costs more than a template with your name pasted in. The difference shows — visitors can feel a template, and it quietly tells them you cut corners.

Content and copy. Someone has to write the words and gather the photos. Good copy — words that actually sell — is skilled work. If a quote seems too cheap, this is usually what's missing, and you'll end up with a beautiful site that says nothing.

Pages and features. Every page is work. Every feature — enquiry forms, WhatsApp buttons, call tracking, online payments, booking calendars — adds build time and testing. Decide what you need before you ask for quotes, or you'll compare prices for different things.

Being found. Basic on-page SEO — proper titles, structure, local signals — should be built in from day one. It's far cheaper than bolting it on later.

Keeping it alive. A domain and hosting cost a small amount every year. That's normal and honest. What isn't normal is a "maintenance contract" that charges you monthly for a site that never changes.

The honest market range

In India today, a simple brochure site from a freelancer can cost a few thousand rupees. A proper custom site from a small studio usually sits in the tens of thousands. Large agencies charge a lakh and up — you're paying for their office as much as your site. None of these is automatically a rip-off; the question is always what you get for it.

Three things you should never pay for

A "premium custom design" that's a ₹2,000 template. Ask to see the design process. If your "custom" site looks like three other businesses' sites, it wasn't custom.

Features you'll never use. A blog you'll never update, a chatbot nobody talks to, twelve pages when four would do. Every unused feature is money you paid to confuse your visitors.

Lock-in. Your domain in someone else's account, your site held hostage by monthly fees, no access to your own content. A website you can't leave isn't yours — walk away from any deal structured like that.

The maths that matters

Stop asking "what does a website cost" and start asking "what does not having one cost". If your business loses even one decent customer a month to a competitor who looks more trustworthy online, the site has paid for itself many times over within a year. A website isn't an expense. It's the cheapest salesperson you'll ever hire — one who works at 2am and never asks for a raise.
