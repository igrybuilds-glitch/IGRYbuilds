Your Instagram isn't a website — why you need both

2026-10-21 · by Manmohan Sharma · ~4 min read

"Why do I need a website? I already have Instagram." I hear this from almost every local business owner I talk to. It's a fair question — Instagram is free, everyone's on it, and it brings real customers. But running your business on Instagram alone is building your shop on someone else's land. Here's the honest breakdown.

Instagram is rented land

You don't own your Instagram account — you're a tenant, and the landlord changes the rules whenever it suits them. The algorithm decides who sees your posts. Accounts get restricted or banned by automated systems with no one to appeal to. I've watched businesses lose years of audience overnight through no fault of their own. Your followers are not your customers until you can reach them without asking a platform's permission.

What Instagram can't do

Be found on Google. When someone in your city searches for what you sell, your Instagram profile is nearly invisible to them. A website shows up. That search traffic is customers with their wallets already half-open — the highest-intent visitors that exist.

Close the deal at 2am. Instagram is a feed, not a front desk. A website answers the three questions every buyer has — what do you do, why should I trust you, how do I contact you — at any hour, without you lifting a finger.

Look serious to serious money. A customer choosing between two similar businesses will trust the one with a real website. It's unfair and it's true: no website reads as "small or temporary" to anyone spending serious money.

What Instagram does better

None of this means Instagram is useless — it's brilliant at what it's built for. Discovery: reels put you in front of people who never searched for you. Personality: customers buy from people they feel they know, and nothing builds that like showing up daily. Speed: you can announce, show, and sell something today, right now. A website can't do any of that as well as Instagram does.

The answer is both, each doing its job

Stop thinking of it as website versus Instagram. The businesses winning locally use both as a system: Instagram brings people in — the discovery, the personality, the daily touch — and the website converts them — the trust, the details, the enquiry at midnight. Your Instagram bio should point to your website, and your website should show your Instagram feed. Each one makes the other work harder.

The real question was never whether you can afford a website. It's whether you can afford to keep your entire business on land you don't own.
